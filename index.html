THE WHOLE THING IN THREE LINES

  1. 半年前我在 B站 landing 靠 coffee chat 和人传人，今天我在 Noumena landing 靠 AI 读完全部周报纪要。差别不是有没有用 AI，是这家公司的知识写没写下来。
  2. 我一个文科生，两个月独立跑通了企业官网的前后端。AI 最被低估的不是它能干活，是它便宜到可以被浪费、可以被无限次否定。
  3. AI 最大的问题不是能力不够，是它不在乎。人的价值在于在乎、审美判断、知道什么时候喊停，以及替 AI 把记忆攒起来。
  1. Six months ago I onboarded at Bilibili through coffee chats and word of mouth. Today I onboard at Noumena by having AI read every weekly report we have ever written. The difference is not whether a company uses AI. It is whether the company writes things down.
  2. I studied humanities. In two months I shipped a corporate website end to end, front and back. The most underrated thing about AI is not that it can do the work. It is that it is cheap enough to waste and can be rejected a hundred times at no cost.
  3. AI's real limitation is not capability. It is that it does not care. Human value now lives in caring, in taste, in knowing when to stop, and in building the memory AI cannot build for itself.

前言PROLOGUE两场 FDE 活动，和一个没被回答的问题Two Meetups, One Unanswered Question

这周我去上海跑了两场 FDE 分享，一场飞书办的，一场千问办公办的。

This week I went to two FDE meetups in Shanghai, one hosted by Feishu and one by Qwen Office.

听完之后我只有一个感受：大家是不是把 FDE 捧得太高了？那种热度我很熟悉，跟 2021 年留学生圈子里「文科转码」的氛围几乎一模一样。一个新词出现，所有人都急着往上贴，但很少有人说清楚它到底解决了什么。

I walked out with one reaction: are we putting FDE on too high a pedestal? The energy felt familiar. It was almost exactly the mood around "humanities majors learning to code" in 2021. A new label appears, everyone rushes to attach themselves to it, and very few people can say what it actually solves.

你要说这两场分享真的让我学到了什么叫 AI Native 的工作模式、看到了颠覆性的企业 AI 革命吗？我要打一个大大的问号。

Did either session teach me what an AI-native way of working really looks like, or show me a genuine enterprise AI revolution? I have serious doubts.

所以我想换个方式回答这个问题。不谈概念，只讲两件我自己从头跑到尾的事。一件关于入职，一件关于做产品。这半年我从 B站换到了一家 AI 创业公司，中间刚好是 coding agent 大规模进入日常工作的半年，对比格外清楚。

So let me answer it differently. No concepts. Just two things I ran end to end myself. One about joining a company, one about building a product. In the past six months I moved from Bilibili to an AI startup, and that window happens to be exactly when coding agents entered daily work at scale. The contrast is unusually clean.

01半年内两次 landing 的对比Two Onboardings, Six Months Apart

同一个人，同样的适应期，完全不同的方式

Same person, same ramp-up period, completely different method

半年前我在 B站 landing。新人上手基本靠 coffee chat、靠张嘴问、靠人传人。今年 CNY 我们推了 100 个直播间，为了让所有内容运营都学会直播运营，开了无数场 3 小时起步、50 人参与的宣讲会。

Six months ago I onboarded at Bilibili. Getting up to speed meant coffee chats, asking around, information passed person to person. For the Chinese New Year push we launched 100 live rooms, and to get every content operator up to speed on livestreaming we ran countless briefing sessions, fifty people each, three hours minimum.

那套流程没有错，它只是很贵。贵在每一次信息传递都要占用一个人的时间，而且传完就散了，不留下任何可复用的东西。

Nothing was wrong with that process. It was just expensive. Expensive because every transfer of information consumed someone's time, and once it was over nothing reusable remained.

今天我加入 Noumena，第一件事是接入飞书 CLI，让 AI 自动读完过往所有周报和周会纪要。我告诉它我负责什么，它按我的职责范围给我列出：该掌握的信息、该精读的文档、日常要对接的上下游 POC。

At Noumena, the first thing I did was connect the Feishu CLI and let AI read every weekly report and meeting note the company had. I told it what I was responsible for, and it mapped my role to a list: what I needed to know, which documents to read closely, and which counterparts I would work with day to day.

但真正起作用的不是工具，是前提。Noumena 从 day one 就保持信息高度透明。所有联创每周向下属汇报，跨部门协同这周做了什么、进度和失败、甚至大家对 AI 的思考，全部被编织进一个巨大的企业 context 里。没有这一层，接什么 CLI 都没用。 The tool is not what made the difference. The precondition did. Noumena has been radically transparent since day one. Every co-founder reports to their team weekly. Every cross-team update, including progress, failures, and half-formed thoughts about AI, gets woven into one enormous company context. Without that layer, no CLI will save you.

这件事我后来想明白了：开会的产出是空气，写下来的产出才是 AI 能吃的 context。一家公司如果所有关键决策都发生在会议室里，它就是一家没有 AI 可用上下文的公司，接再强的模型也没用，因为知识全在人脑子里。

Here is what I eventually understood. What happens in a meeting is air. What gets written down is context AI can actually consume. If all your key decisions live in meeting rooms, you are a company with no usable AI context. Plug in the strongest model available and it will not help, because the knowledge never left anyone's head.

AI Native 这件事，真的跟年龄无关，跟你愿不愿意把自己的判断标准写下来有关。
Why this line matters: we assume only young digital natives can be truly AI-native. But this came from someone with two decades in the industry. He was not "learning AI." He had reorganised human-to-human communication around how AI works.

Being AI-native has nothing to do with age. It has everything to do with whether you are willing to write your own judgment criteria down.

更日常的场景是开会。所有会议我都开飞书妙记，结束后把纪要直接丢给 AI，它帮我改好，不需要任何额外的 prompt。真正的变化不在于省了整理时间，而在于会议的产出物从「一份纪要」变成了「一批带归属人的任务」。纪要没人看，任务有人认。

A more everyday case: meetings. I record everything with Feishu Minutes, then hand the transcript to AI, which cleans it up without needing a prompt. The real change is not the time saved on formatting. It is that the output of a meeting shifts from "a set of notes" to "a set of tasks with owners." Nobody reads notes. People claim tasks.

02文科生独立跑通企业官网前后端A Humanities Major Shipping a Full Stack Alone

两个月，四次大版本迭代，一个人

Two months, four major iterations, one person

这两个月我上线了职业生涯里自己 own 的最大项目：公司的新版官网。前后经历四次大的版本迭代，才成了现在的样子。期间我看了无数企业官网，包括 Palantir 和 Ploy.ai，拆了一堆交互模式，试了好几种视觉方向。

Over the past two months I shipped the largest project I have ever owned: our company's new website. It took four major iterations to get here. Along the way I studied countless corporate sites, including Palantir and Ploy.ai, dissected their interaction patterns, and tried several visual directions.

这件事放在半年前，我绝对不敢想。

Six months ago I would not have dared.

从我为了转行 AI 手搓个人官网，到加入公司之后独立打通企业官网的前后端链路，中间只隔了半年。你们能想象一个文科生自己跑通前后端吗？我半年前也想不到。

From hand-building a personal site to pivot into AI, to shipping a corporate site front and back on my own, the gap was six months. A humanities major running her own full stack. I could not have pictured it either.

最关键的一步其实不是前端。前端做得再漂亮，也只是一张好看的静态页。真正让它变成一个产品的是那条后端链路：访客填完留资表单，数据直接落进飞书表格，销售当天就能跟进。那一刻它才从「作品」变成了「在运转的东西」。

The decisive step was not the front end. However good it looks, a front end alone is still a pretty static page. What made it a product was the back end: a visitor fills in the form, the data lands directly in a Feishu table, and sales follows up the same day. That is the moment it stopped being a piece of work and became something that runs.

03总结：人与 AI 到底差在哪Conclusion: Where Humans and AI Actually Differ

六个优势，四条边界，和一套协作方法

Six advantages, four limits, and a way to work together

把这两件事放在一起看，我想清楚了一件事：大部分人低估 AI，不是低估了它的能力，是低估了它和人在结构上的不同。下面这六条，才是真正改变工作方式的地方。

Putting both cases side by side, one thing became clear. Most people underestimate AI not by misjudging its capability, but by missing how structurally unlike a human it is. These six properties are where the real change happens.

AI 相对于人的六个优势

Six ways AI is structurally unlike a human

01并发Parallelism

人是单线程的，AI 可以同时开二十个。一个人做不到「同时试五个方案再挑最好的」，AI 可以。

Humans are single-threaded. AI can run twenty tracks at once. No person can try five approaches simultaneously and then pick the best. AI can.

意味着什么：你的角色从操作者变成了调度者和验收者。这不是效率提升，是工种改变。
What it means: your role shifts from operator to dispatcher and reviewer. That is not a productivity gain. It is a different job.

02上下文吞吐量Context throughput

一次读完五十万字，然后回答关于其中任意一处的细节问题。人做不到，而且这不是靠训练能补上的差距。

It reads half a million words at once and then answers a question about any detail inside them. No human can, and no amount of training closes that gap.

我的实例：让它读完公司一年的周报和纪要，给我列出该掌握什么。人要花几周，它几分钟。
My case: it read a year of company reports and told me what I needed to know. Weeks of human work, done in minutes.

03零社交成本Zero social cost

很多事人不做，不是因为做不到，是因为要麻烦别人、要欠人情、要问一个显得自己很蠢的问题。这层摩擦被彻底取消了。

Plenty of things go undone not because people cannot do them, but because doing them means bothering someone, owing a favour, or asking a question that makes you look stupid. That friction is gone.

意味着什么：释放出来的量比想象中大得多，尤其对新人和跨部门协作。
What it means: the unlock is far larger than it sounds, especially for new joiners and cross-team work.

04宽度Breadth

AI 让一个人的覆盖面积突然变大。它在任何单项上都不是最强的，但能帮你跑通一个项目的完整闭环。

AI expands the surface area one person can cover. It is not the best at any single thing, but it can carry an entire project loop end to end.

我的实例：文案、视觉、前端、后端、部署，五个环节以前要五个人。
My case: copy, visuals, front end, back end, deployment. Five roles before. One person now.

05没有情绪损耗No emotional cost

你跟同事说第三次「不对重来」，关系就裂了。你跟 AI 说 100 次，它下一次还是笑眯眯地给你干。

Tell a colleague "wrong, start over" a third time and something breaks. Tell AI a hundred times and it comes back exactly as willing.

我的实例：调官网动效那两周，我说的「不对」比这半年跟人说的都多。
My case: during two weeks of tuning animations I said "not right" more times than I had to humans all year.

06便宜到可以浪费Cheap enough to waste

10 万的外包预算，变成了两个月 Claude 200 刀的 plan，不到 3000 块，外加我个人的时间。

A six-figure RMB agency budget became two months on a $200 Claude plan, under 3,000 RMB, plus my own time.

真正的变化：不是省了钱，是做四版再扔掉三版这件事，第一次变得不心疼。
The real shift: not the savings. It is that building four versions and discarding three stopped hurting.
第六条才是真正的分水岭。人的时间贵，所以人做事必须先规划、一次做对。AI 便宜到可以先做三版扔掉两版。功夫从「想清楚再动手」转移到了「快速判断哪版更好」。你的方法论要跟着改，否则你买了最快的工具，还在用最慢的流程。 The sixth is the real watershed. Human time is expensive, so humans must plan first and get it right once. AI is cheap enough to build three and throw two away. The effort moves from thinking it through to judging quickly which version is better. Your method has to change too, or you have bought the fastest tool and kept the slowest process.

但它有四条过不去的边界

But it has four hard limits

用了这么久，我发现 AI 最大的问题不是能力不够，是它不在乎。

After all this time, AI's biggest problem is not capability. It is that it does not care.

它不在乎

It does not care

它不承担后果，所以它说「小心」的时候，是在模仿小心，不是真的怕。你让它写一封发给 10 万用户的推送，它写得很快，但不会多想一秒「这句话发出去会不会出事」。

It bears no consequences, so when it says "be careful" it is imitating caution, not feeling it. Ask it to write a push notification going to 100,000 users and it writes fast, without spending one second on whether that line could blow up.

它的审美是平均数

Its taste is an average

做出来的东西你看着觉得「嗯，都对」，但不会有人惊呼「卧槽这个好」。那个天花板，得靠人去顶。

What it produces reads as "yes, all correct." Nobody gasps. That ceiling has to be pushed by a human.

它不会说「这事根本不该做」

It will not tell you to stop

你给了它一个错的方向，它会沿着错的方向跑得非常漂亮，最后交付一个精美的错误。

Point it in the wrong direction and it will run that direction beautifully, delivering an immaculate mistake.

它记不住

It does not remember

你以为它记住了你上个月说的偏好，其实它没有。它的记忆是你一砖一瓦搭起来的，你得替它建，它不会自己长出来。

You think it remembers the preference you stated last month. It does not. Its memory is something you build brick by brick. It will not grow one on its own.

所以，怎么才能更好地协作

So how do you actually work with it

把上面六条优势和四条边界对齐，协作方式其实很清楚：让 AI 承担它结构上占优的部分，把人放回「承担后果」的那一侧。

Line up the six advantages against the four limits and the answer is straightforward. Give AI the parts where it is structurally superior, and put humans back on the side that bears consequences.

原则具体怎么做
把知识写下来开会的产出是空气，写下来的才是 AI 能吃的 context。先解决「有没有」，再谈「用什么模型」
把判断标准也写下来一份 agent.md，把你关心什么、怎么判断、讨厌什么写清楚。别人可以先跟你的 agent 对齐，再来占用你的时间
